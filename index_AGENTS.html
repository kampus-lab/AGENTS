01 · The situation, January 2024

An early mover suddenly has company

Who

Married co-founders Tatjana Samsonowa (CEO; ex-SAP researcher, PhD in information systems, former professor) and Sebastian Denef (CTO & product; ex-Fraunhofer researcher, PhD in industrial design).

What they sell

AI agents that do knowledge work: they monitor news, competitors, partners, patents and regulation, and deliver verified reports, alerts and dashboards.

Track record

First enterprise client in 2020. Customers include Porsche, Brazil's Petrobras and the German Federal Ministry of Labor.

The problem

ChatGPT's launch in late 2022 made "AI agents" a hot category. VC-backed startups and big tech are moving in, and the founders believe the platform choice will be settled within about two years.

“You are either the platform people use, or you are not.”Sebastian Denef, co-founder
02 · What the founders must decide

Three linked decisions

Decision 1

Open source?

Should they release part of their technology as open source software? Which part, and under what licence, so the company still makes money?

Decision 2

Funding

They need a seed round to keep pace with VC-backed rivals. How much, and how will the open-source choice affect what investors think?

Decision 3

Business model

Keep selling agent contracts to large companies, or bet on an agent store and platform that others build on?

The decisions pull against each other: what helps build a developer community may worry enterprise customers and change which investors are interested.

As you readKeep these three decisions in mind. Every slide that follows gives you evidence for one or more of them, so note which way each fact pushes.
Glossary · terms you will meet in the case

Key terms in plain language

LLM (large language model)

The AI engine behind tools like ChatGPT. It predicts and generates text.

Hallucination

When an AI states something false with confidence.

On-premises

Software and data run on the customer's own servers instead of a vendor's cloud.

Tokens (as pricing)

Credits a customer buys in advance and spends each time an agent does a task.

Open source software (OSS)

Software whose code is public, so anyone can read, change and reuse it.

Licence

The legal terms that say what others may do with the code, e.g. whether they can sell it.

SDK (software development kit)

A free toolkit that lets outside developers build on a company's platform.

Seed round / VC

A startup's first outside funding, usually from venture capital firms that take shares in return.

Platform / network effects

A platform gets more valuable as more users and builders join, like an app store.

B2B

Business-to-business: selling to companies rather than to consumers.

03 · Concept

What the case means by an “AI agent”

A program that receives a goal, makes its own decisions, and takes actions to reach it, without a human re-prompting it at each step.

Generative AI (chatbot)

Suggests a travel itinerary or a restaurant.

You still do the booking. Each new step needs a new prompt.

AI agent

Books the flight and orders the food, then checks its own results.

Agents can run tasks in parallel, hand work to other agents and combine the results.

Think about itPick a task you do every week that takes several apps and several steps, such as planning a trip or preparing a group assignment. Which steps could an agent do for you, and which would you still want to check yourself?
04 · Case Exhibit 1, simplified

How an AGENTS.inc agent works

1

Goal

A user sets a standing task, e.g. “Watch my competitors.”

2

Gather data

Pulls the client's internal data plus validated public sources.

3

Pick models

Chooses which AI models to use: LLMs, sentiment, entity recognition and others.

4

Sort output

Compares and groups what the different models return.

5

Validate

Checks results against the data and adds citations.

6

Report

Sends reports, alerts and live dashboards.

↺The agent keeps monitoring the goal and repeats the cycle. It can also ask a helper agent (for example, a news agent) to work on part of the task.

Why no known hallucinations, according to the company: each prompt carries so much context that the model has little room to invent things.

05 · Background from the case

A quick map of AI terms

By capability
  • Narrow (“weak”) AI: trained for one task. Everything in use today.
  • Artificial general intelligence: a wide range of tasks at human level or above.
  • Super AI: theoretical; would reason and learn beyond humans.
By functionality
  • Reactive: no memory, one fixed task.
  • Limited memory: recalls past data. LLMs, Siri, self-driving cars.
  • Theory of mind and self-aware: theoretical future stages.

07 · A pattern in the case

Each crisis brought new customers

2020 · COVID-19

Pharma supply chains

A Fortune 500 pharmaceutical company, reached through an SAP contact, used agents to evaluate new suppliers and partners.

2020–22 · Brazil

Petrobras politics

After a change of government pushed the state oil company toward renewables, agents tracked every relevant speech, news item and post. Reporting went from daily to every 10 minutes.

2022 · Ukraine war

Supply and regulation

Companies facing sudden shifts in suppliers, rules and public relations signed up.

Think about itWhy is an always-on monitoring agent most valuable when the world is unstable? If most new customers arrive because of a crisis, is that a strength or a risk for the business?

09 · Competitive advantage

Why customers chose a tiny startup

Many models, not one

Each task runs through several AI models and the results are compared. Customers don't want to depend on a single provider.

Data stays at home

Agents can run on the client's own servers. The Bavarian state police required this, and German firms value it highly.

Built for ordinary employees

An agent can be set up in three clicks, with no IT department or consultant needed.

Verified and cited

Every report carries sources, and the agents comply with EU privacy rules.

Tailored

Agents are configured to each client's industry and use case.

Persistence

As a small vendor, they made themselves indispensable so clients would take the risk.

13 · Concept: open source software

Free as in freedom, not as in price

Open source software ships with its readable source code, so anyone can study, modify, fix and share it. Examples: Linux, Android, Firefox, the Apache web server.

  • Copyleft (e.g. GNU GPL): changes must stay open; limits making it proprietary
  • Permissive (e.g. MIT): anyone can use it for anything, including commercial products

By 2023, 96% of codebases contained open source. Red Hat sold its open-source OS to over 90% of the Fortune 500. Microsoft paid $7.5B for GitHub.

14 · Decision 1

What to open, and how far

Option A

Agent-building kit

Let anyone build their own agents. Could be a free SDK rather than true open source, like Apple's developer tools.

Option B

“Linux for agents”

Open the runtime that runs agents. Big global reach; work out the business model later.

Option C

Store infrastructure

Open the marketplace itself. Very complex, and Apple and Google keep their stores closed.

Most control

Restrictive custom licence

Free for students and internal use; no competing stores; limits by company size or use case.

Middle

Free SDK, closed core

Developers build agents; the platform and store stay proprietary.

Most reach

MIT licence for everything

The “big, bold step.” Anyone can use it commercially. Is there a community, and how does the firm earn money?

For

Clients want to run the platform themselves, like WordPress for agents. The team loves open source, and the world will need more niche agents than four people can build.

Against

Big firms may hear “open source” as risk. There are no resources to build a community, and the technology is complex to set up.

15 · Decision 2

Raise $5 million or $50 million?

$5M plan

Build sales and marketing, hire developers, start scaling the current business.

$50M plan

Needed if the bet is on the agent store. Samsonowa is talking to both kinds of investors.

Customer-first culture

Profitable and grown on customer revenue; the CEO prefers proving value over borrowing.

German context

Investors favour thoroughly tested, reliable products over speed to market. US investors take more risk.

Network gap

No serial founder who can call investors and raise quickly. Denef believes the idea itself could attract $100M.

Link to Decision 1: a small group of VCs specialises in open-source companies. Going open source changes which investors will listen, and on what terms.

Think about itInvestors take a share of the company in return for money. Why might founders of a profitable firm hesitate to raise money at all? What do they risk by not raising?
16 · Decision 3

Stay a B2B vendor or become the platform?

B2B contracts (today)
  • Proven revenue from large clients
  • Full control over quality
  • Plan for 2024: grow monthly revenue, more agents per client, add smaller clients
  • Focus narrowly on corporate intelligence, where verified results matter
Agent store & platform
  • Store revenue is low today
  • Unknown whether the market will be winner-take-all, like app stores
  • Network effects could reward the first big platform
  • Must vet every third-party agent, and control passes to many hands
  • Vision: ready-made data on every company and news source

Cost pressure: matching data and comparing models means about a million web requests where a typical AI use makes hundreds, so data collection and storage grow in importance.

Think about itCompare with app stores you use. Apple and Google control theirs and earn from every app. What would AGENTS.inc need to make a store work, and could a four-person firm manage it?

Questions for the case discussion

Jot down a short answer to each. There is no single right answer; what matters is backing your view with evidence from the case.

  2. Which of its advantages (multiple models, on-premises data, citations, ease of use) can big tech copy most easily, and which are hardest to copy?

19 · Key takeaways

What to remember about agentic AI

Agents act, chatbots answer

The value is in completing and verifying multi-step work, often continuously.

Trust is the product

Citations, validation, data sovereignty and multi-model checks matter as much as raw capability.

Pricing is new territory

Agents consume compute and data per task, so token-based and dynamic pricing replace simple licences.

Platform battles move fast

Open versus closed, store versus service, and funding strategy decide who wins, not just technology.
